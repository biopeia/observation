# Ontogeny, Reproduction, and Lineage: The Boundaries of Embodiment

The life cycle of a Symbiont organism traces a path from materialization through physical maturation, eventual senescence, and the potential propagation of its lineage. An organism that secures sufficient resources and health may reproduce. During Genome v2 resident budding, the child receives the unchanged parental genome (without recombination or allele mutation) and does not inherit the parent's acquired memories, neural topologies, or cognitive connections. An explicit `EpigeneticProtocol` may separately admit germline marks for transmission and child birth expression; this mechanical path does not establish adaptive benefit. Somatic `EpigeneticPrior` is not implicitly converted into germline state.

This separation reflects a deep systemic divide at the embodiment boundary, where physical development is strictly decoupled from cognitive learning. The physical trajectory of the body—tracking `growth_progress`, physical maturity, and eventual decline—is governed by the `OntogenyController`. In stark contrast, cognitive maturity and the accumulation of knowledge are managed independently by the `DevelopmentalTracker`. Consequently, intellectual sophistication does not confer reproductive viability, just as physical readiness does not imply cognitive mastery.

The drive to propagate is meticulously regulated. An organism evaluates its reproductive readiness based on stringent physical criteria encapsulated within its `LivingBodyState`. It must reach full physical maturity, where `growth_progress` equals exactly 1.0. Furthermore, it requires a substantial surplus of energy; the reserves tracked within its `MetabolicLedger` must unequivocally exceed the minimum reproductive threshold. Finally, structural integrity is paramount—the physical body cannot be in a state of critical damage. Throughout this rigorous physiological assessment, the organism's cognitive state, predictive successes, or rich conceptual topologies exert absolutely no influence on the boolean determination of its readiness to reproduce.

Internal readiness alone does not create offspring. Laboratory orchestration calls `lab.reproduction.materialize_clonal_bud(parent, authority=authority)`, checks the parent's physiological readiness and external capacity, and obtains a slot from the Lab-owned `HabitatBirthAuthority`. A capacity denial leaves the parent's energy unchanged. The subject neither retains population authority nor constructs its descendant. Laboratory callers also observe death and release population slots explicitly.

During clonal budding, the child receives the unchanged parent's Genome v2 and a newly initialized cognitive graph; learned concepts and synaptic weights are not inherited. Separately, the parent's inheritance package may carry only germline marks admitted by its explicit `EpigeneticProtocol`. Those marks can affect the child's birth expression, but tests establish mechanics rather than adaptive benefit. This contract does not convert somatic `EpigeneticPrior` state into germline inheritance.
